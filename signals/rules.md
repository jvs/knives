# Signals

A cooperative trick-taking card game for 4 players.

---

## Overview

Signals is a cooperative trick-taking game. Each round, the players face a challenge together. They must coordinate their play to meet the challenge's objective — but they can't freely discuss their hands. Instead, they communicate through careful card play and the Signals suit, a small set of cards that let you speak a few precious words when played.

## Components

- **28 standard cards:** Four suits (clubs, diamonds, hearts, spades), each with 7 cards ranked low to high: Ghost, Prank, Maid, Jack, Queen, King, Ace.
- **4 Prank ability cards:** One per suit. Each Prank card has a special ability that triggers when played.
- **3 Signal cards:** Numbered 2, 3, and 4. A fifth suit called Signals.
- **1 Joker:** A special card whose ability changes each round.
- **3 Joker reference cards (double-sided):** Used to indicate the Joker's current ability.
- **A challenge booklet:** A collection of challenges the players attempt to complete.

**Total deck size: 32 cards.**

## The Deck in Detail

### Standard Cards

Each of the four suits contains seven cards. From lowest to highest rank:

Ghost — Prank — Maid — Jack — Queen — King — Ace

### Prank Cards

Each suit's Prank card has a special ability. Prank cards are also normal suited cards with a rank (between Ghost and Maid) and must follow all standard trick-taking rules.

| Suit | Name | Ability |
|------|------|---------|
| Clubs | The Cat | You lead the next trick, regardless of who wins the current trick. |
| Diamonds | The Devil | Select another player to swap cards with. Each of you selects a card from your hand and exchanges them face-down. You may also swap with a shadow card (face-up or face-down). |
| Hearts | The Hound | Select another player. They reveal one card of their choice to all players, keeping it in hand. You may instead flip one face-down shadow card face-up. |
| Spades | The Snitch | Name a card (e.g., "King of Hearts"). If any player holds it, they must reveal it to all players, keeping it in hand. |

**Prank timing:** Prank abilities take effect when the card is played (before the trick resolves), except for the Cat, which takes effect after the trick resolves. Prank cards have no special effect on the final trick of the round.

### Signal Cards

The Signal suit contains three cards: the 2, 3, and 4 of Signals.

**When you play a Signal card, you may speak a number of words equal to the card's rank.** For example, if you play the 3 of Signals, you may say up to three words (e.g., "I have spades"). Contractions count as one word. You can say anything — there are no restrictions on content.

Signal cards follow normal suit-following rules. You may only play a Signal card if Signals were led or if you are void in the suit that was led. Leading a Signal card is legal and forces other players holding Signals to follow suit.

### The Joker

There is one Joker card in the deck. Its ability changes each round, chosen by the players before the deal. The Joker can always be played on any trick regardless of what suit was led.

When leading with the Joker, name a suit. That suit is considered the led suit for the trick.

If the Joker's current ability does not specify whether it wins or loses, it has the lowest rank and cannot win the trick.

**Joker Abilities:**

| Name | Ability |
|------|---------|
| The Captain | Name a rank (or suit). All players reveal any cards of that rank (or suit). |
| The Hypnotist | Ask one player a single yes-or-no question about their hand. |
| The Juggler | Each player passes one card to the player on their left. |
| The Magician | All players reveal one card from their hand. Flip one face-down shadow card face-up. |
| The Ringleader | Choose which player leads the next trick. |
| The Trickster | Always wins the trick. |

Use the double-sided Joker reference cards to indicate the current ability. Place the selected reference card face-up on the table next to the challenge.

## Setup

1. **Select a challenge** from the booklet.
2. **Choose the Joker's ability.** The table discusses and agrees on a Joker ability for the round. Place the corresponding reference card face-up on the table.
3. **Shuffle and deal.** Shuffle all 32 cards. Deal 7 cards to each player. Place the remaining 4 cards face-down in the center of the table — this is **the shadows**. Flip 2 of the 4 shadow cards face-up.
4. **Check the shadows.** If the face-up shadow cards may make the current challenge impossible, shuffle and redeal, or select a new challenge.

## Playing a Round

Signals follows standard trick-taking rules.

### Leading

The dealer leads the first trick. Play a card from your hand face-up to the center of the table. The suit of this card is the **led suit** for the trick.

### Following

Moving clockwise, each player plays one card. **You must follow suit if able** — if you have a card matching the led suit, you must play one. If you are void in the led suit, you may play any card.

### Winning the Trick

The highest card in the **led suit** wins the trick, unless a trump card was played. **Spades are always trump** unless the players have opted for a "no trump" challenge, in which case there are no trump cards.

If one or more trump cards were played by players who are void in the led suit, the highest trump wins instead.

The winner of the trick collects the played cards and places them face-down in front of them. The winner leads the next trick.

**Exception:** If the Cat (Prank of Clubs) was played, the player who played it leads the next trick instead of the trick's winner. If the Mastermind Joker was played, the Joker's player chooses who leads next.

### Signals Suit

Signal cards are a normal suit for the purposes of following suit. If Signals are led, players holding Signal cards must play them. Signal cards cannot be trump.

### End of Round

After all 7 tricks have been played, check whether the challenge objective was met. If so, the players win. If not, they may try again with a new deal, or select a new challenge.

## Challenges

The challenge booklet contains 40 challenges across four difficulty levels. Each challenge specifies an objective the players must collectively achieve. Players may optionally increase the difficulty of a challenge by declaring "no trump," meaning spades have no special power for that round.

Players can approach challenges however they like — pick one at random, try one that looks fun, or work through them in order. There is no required campaign or progression.

### Beginner

1. **Abundance:** Every player must win at least one trick.
2. **Nil:** At least one player must win no tricks.
3. **Alpha Omega:** The player who wins the first trick must also win the last trick.
4. **Harmless Pranks:** No Prank card may win a trick.
5. **Front-Row Seats:** No player may win more than one Prank card.
6. **The Emperor Has No Clothes:** No King may win a trick.
7. **Amateur Hour:** No Ace may win a trick.
8. **Let's Get Serious:** Players must not play any Prank cards in the first four tricks.

### Intermediate

9. **Fussy Nil:** Exactly one player must win no tricks.
10. **Poor Dealer:** The dealer must not win any tricks.
11. **Take Turns:** No player may win two tricks in a row.
12. **Generosity:** No trick is won by the player who led it.
13. **Aces All Around:** No player may win more than one Ace.
14. **Flying Aces:** No Ace may lose a trick.
15. **Kingly Crowns:** No King may lose a trick.
16. **Even Steven:** Three players win two tricks each. One player wins one trick.
17. **Comedy of Errors:** Once a player plays a Prank card, they must not win any remaining tricks.
18. **Clown Car:** Only one player may win tricks containing Prank cards.
19. **Royalty:** One trick must be won by an Ace, one by a King, and one by a Queen.
20. **Misfire:** At least one Prank card must win a trick.

### Advanced

21. **Neighborhood:** When a player wins a trick, the player to their left or right must win the next trick.
22. **Keep Away:** When a player wins a trick, they name a player. That player must not win the next trick.
23. **Passing Game:** When a player wins a trick, they name a player. That player must win the next trick.
24. **Heated Rivalry:** One player must win three tricks. Another must win four.
25. **Five:** One player must win five tricks.
26. **Fibonacci:** Two players win one trick each, one player wins two, and one player wins three.
27. **Bedlam:** At least two Prank cards must win tricks.
28. **Always Room for Dessert:** When a player wins their first trick, they must also win the next trick.
29. **Ladies First:** At least two Queens must each win a trick.
30. **Jack Attack:** At least two Jacks must each win a trick.
31. **Haunted House:** Only one player may win tricks containing Ghost cards.

### Expert

32. **Clockwork:** When a player wins a trick, the player to their left must win the next trick.
33. **Anti-Clockwork:** When a player wins a trick, the player to their right must win the next trick.
34. **Even Dealer Steven:** The dealer wins exactly one trick. Each other player wins two.
35. **Perfect Fibonacci:** The first player to win a trick wins exactly one. The second player to win a trick wins exactly one. The third player to win a trick wins two. The remaining player wins the rest.
36. **Six:** One player must win six tricks.
37. **Prediction Time:** Before the first trick, starting with the dealer, each player predicts how many tricks they will win. Each player must win exactly the number they predicted.
38. **Foolish Consistency:** Each trick must not contain more than two different suits, except for the last trick.
39. **Menagerie:** No trick may contain two cards of the same rank.
40. **Memory Trick:** Each player must win at least one trick. After the round, players must collectively name both face-down shadow cards. If correct, they win.

### Challenge Modifiers

Each challenge may be made more difficult by adding a modifier:
- no trump: spades are not trump for this round. The highest card in the led suit wins the trick, even if a spade is played.
- no signals: signal cards do not let you speak when played. They are just normal cards of a fifth suit.
- no joke: the joker is treated as the zero of hearts. It has no special ability and is not a trump card.

## Tips for New Players

- **Pay attention to what's void.** When a player can't follow suit, that tells you something about their hand. This is free information — use it.
- **Signal cards are precious.** Don't waste them. A well-timed two-word hint can win the round.
- **Leading a Signal is a bold move.** It forces anyone holding Signals to follow suit, potentially opening up multiple lines of communication in one trick. But it also burns through the team's limited Signals quickly.
- **The Prank cards work differently here than in competitive games.** The Devil isn't an attack — it's a way to get the right card to the right player. The Hound isn't an intrusion — it's a gift of information. Think cooperatively.
- **Don't forget the shadows.** Two cards are hidden. Your plan might fail because a key card isn't in play. Use the Hound or the Mentalist to learn what's buried.
